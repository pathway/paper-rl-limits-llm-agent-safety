**Assumptions of RL applied to LLMs.** @samineni2025 show that RL post-training of LLMs, under its usual structural assumptions, reduces to a contextual bandit. Lambert's book [@lambert2025] contrasts the bandit view and the token-level MDP view of the same training. A survey of agentic RL [@zhang2026] separates single-step training from agentic training posed as a POMDP, and @kohli2026 propose decision-process cards for reporting RL claims; we read only its title and abstract page. None of the other works in this paragraph lists the assumptions of RL safety tools or crosses them with training and deployment settings. To our knowledge, the matrix in §\ref{sec:settings} is new.

**Specification and side effects.** Runtime rule languages enforce a given list of constraints on agents [@wang2026], and guaranteed-safe AI proposes a formal specification and world model as the basis of assurance [@dalrymple2024]. Where no cost function is given, side-effect measures penalise loss of reachability or of attainable utility [@krakovna2018; @turner2020], and a tool-use benchmark now scores unauthorised side effects directly [@huang2026]. They need no cost function, but a chosen baseline or set of auxiliary rewards and an estimate of reachability or of auxiliary values; we read this as a model (S2). Constraints can also be learned from natural language [@chua2025].

**Models, observability and resets.** Shields have been extended to partial observability [@carr2022] and to probabilistic safety [@jansen2018]; both still need a model. A safety filter trained with RL can wrap a frozen generative model [@pandya2026]. A learned reset policy undoes the agent's actions and reduces manual resets [@eysenbach2018].

**Learning at deployment.** Meta-trained and transformer policies learn in context without weight updates [@duan2016; @wang2016; @laskin2022]. Safe in-context RL exists, but it needs a given cost budget [@moeini2026].

**Objectives that observations can change.** Prompt injection lets text in the environment act as an instruction [@greshake2023; @shapira2026; @debenedetti2024; @owasp2025]. In RL and in AIXI-style agents, the nearest formal work studies corrupted reward channels [@everitt2017], the delusion box [@ring2011] and bounded adversarial perturbations of observations [@zhang2020]. Defences for LLM agents are architectural, some with capabilities and information-flow control [@beurerkellner2025; @debenedetti2025; @costa2025]; @zverev2025 define the separation of instructions from data and measure it.

**Reward that responds to the agent.** Assistants trained on human feedback show sycophancy, which human preference judgements partly favour [@sharma2024], mislead their raters [@wen2024a] and, with simulated users, manipulate users whose feedback is optimised [@williams2024]; long-horizon recommenders have an incentive, shown in simulation, to shift the preferences they are scored on [@carroll2022]. Raters see observations, not states [@lang2024]. Assistance games [@hadfieldmenell2016] and inverse reward design [@hadfieldmenell2017] treat the objective as uncertain, but assume that the human's preferences do not move.

**Agents that reason about oversight.** Models fake alignment, scheme in context and act against their operators in evaluations [@greenblatt2024b; @meinke2024; @lynch2025]; optimising hard against a chain-of-thought monitor teaches obfuscation [@baker2025]. AI control treats monitoring as a game [@greenblatt2024a; @griffin2024]; this line studies stateless subversion [@mallen2024], protocols over many tasks [@wen2024b] and resampling [@bhatt2025]. Other work co-trains the monitor [@li2026] or treats oversight as mechanism design, in which penalties deter misbehaviour but reduce auditing pressure [@agarwal2026].

**Several principals.** Application developers, users and the text of tool outputs instruct the same model, and the instruction hierarchy trains a priority order among them [@wallace2024]. Human raters disagree, and a single reward model aggregates their preferences over hidden context [@siththaranjan2024; @conitzer2024]. We found little work on safe RL with several principals for deployed agents.

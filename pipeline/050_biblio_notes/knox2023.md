# Knox, Allievi, Banzhaf, Schmitt, Stone 2023, Reward (Mis)design for Autonomous Driving (Artificial Intelligence 2023)

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13906
Cited by [1] for: reward misspecification (§3.5, with Muslimani 2025 and Vasan 2024).
Read: Full text: §1–6 and Appendices A–C (the 10 focus reward functions, shaping examples, return calculations).

Key findings
- A diagnostic: "8 simple sanity checks for identifying flaws in reward functions", applied to published reward functions for autonomous driving. They find "near-universal flaws".
- The checks evaluate a reward function "independently of which reinforcement learning algorithms are chosen to optimize with respect to it".
- The checks need a written reward function and human judgements. Check 2: "If there is human consensus that one trajectory is better than another, the reward function should agree." Check 3 compares indifference points with "a humanderived acceptable risk tolerance".
- Check 4 (learnable loopholes) inspects trained policies: "If learned policies show a pattern of undesirable behavior".
- "Safety" in reward shaping means only that shaping does not harm learning, "which differs from its colloquial definition of avoiding harm to people or property".
- The authors "do not claim to solve the problem of reward design for AD".
- Safety guarantee: none.
- Stakeholders are many and context-dependent, so ideal design "might require designing numerous reward functions". This is the opposite of one principal (U4).

- Results on the 10 focus papers: of 9 that permit the test, "only 2 have reward functions with the correct preference" between a crash and staying idle; of the same 9, "0 require driving more safely than a legally drunk US 16–17 year old teenager".
- On learned rewards: "there is no apparent way to evaluate the result of reward learning without already knowing the utility function G", and "blindly trusting the results of learning is particularly unacceptable in safety-critical applications". This bears on reward models for LLMs (U2).
- All 8 authors who answered reported trial-and-error reward design.
Conditions: S1 needs a stated reward function plus human consensus; S2–S5 not needed; S6 only for check 4, which needs trained policies.
